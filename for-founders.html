Senior Product Designer

You can hire a senior designer in three to six months. You are shipping this quarter.

Charles Berg · charles@crb.is · San Mateo, CA

01The challenge

You can afford a senior designer. The problem is time. A senior design hire takes a quarter or two to find, longer to onboard, and you are shipping now. So the design gets done by a founder at midnight, or by an engineer who is guessing, or by a contractor who hands over a file and leaves.

The design decisions get made either way.

There is a second problem underneath the first: most teams at your stage are not yet sure what the full-time role should be. Systems designer, product designer, researcher, all three in one person. Working together is the fastest way to find out, and it costs you nothing you were not already going to spend.

02How it works

We can work in three ways.

A

Burst

Full time, four to six weeks, one outcome. Stand up a design system, get a product to launch, unblock a release. Hard stop on a date we set at the start. For a team with a deadline and a budget that ends with it.

B

Embedded

Up to twenty hours a week, month to month. For a team carrying an open design role while the search runs. This offers stability and continuity.

C

Office hours

Four to eight hours a week. For a team whose engineers keep running into design decisions they need help with. I run this now for a Fortune 500 design organization.

Whichever shape: I am in your standups, your Figma, and the Slack thread where the real decision gets made.

I take one embedded engagement at a time.

03The first ninety days

First thirty days
I am in your standups and your Figma from week one. I go through what you already have and ship something small and visible early, so the team can see what changes when a designer is in the room.
By day sixty
The decisions you keep relitigating have a pattern instead of a debate. The small system starts here, built out of the screens we actually shipped rather than in the abstract.
By day ninety
Your engineers can handle the settled parts without me, and I am on the new hard things. If you have hired by then, I hand over to them and leave the patterns behind.

04What a team gets

  • Product design end to end, from research through shipped screens
  • Someone senior enough to push back on the roadmap, not just execute it
  • Working prototypes, not just mockups. I build with AI agents, so ideas get tested before they get built.
  • Accessibility built in rather than bolted on. The most recent chapter of my career was platform accessibility at Google, and it costs far less here than after an enterprise buyer's procurement team asks for a VPAT.
  • Patterns and a small system your engineers can keep using after I'm gone
  • A clearer answer to what your first full-time design hire should actually be

05Who I am

A product designer and leader with over two decades of design at Google, Microsoft, and Motorola.

Until recently, Staff UX Designer on Android and Pixel, where I led Magnifier (154K monthly users, 4.8 stars, highest in category), Simple View (80 percent in-store conversion), and Guided Step, an AI navigation tool for blind users that cut obstacle hits by 44 percent in study. Earlier at Google I designed the four-light pattern on every Google Home speaker, now on 25 million devices, and Google.org's Amazon deforestation dashboard, co-designed with the forest monitors who use it. At Microsoft I led eight designers through Skype's mobile redesign.

My specialty is interfaces with no standard screen to lean on. That turns out to describe most of the hard problems.

06Terms

$200 per hour

Tell me the hours you have and the date you need, and I will tell you what fits inside them.

Monthly invoicing, thirty days notice in either direction. No retainer minimum and no long contract, because if it isn't working you should be able to stop.

Give me thirty minutes.

I'll look at what you have built and tell you what I would do first. That is useful to you whether or not we work together.

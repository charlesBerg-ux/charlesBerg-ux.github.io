What I do

Three offerings, woven together.

UX Design, Co-Design Facilitation, and Strategic Advisory. They aren't separate product lines. A UX engagement often includes a co-design series; an advisory often recommends one as the next step. Below is what each looks like in practice.

  • 01 · UX Design
  • 02 · Co-Design Facilitation
  • 03 · Strategic Advisory

01 · UX Design

Staff-level design, on contract.

Staff-level means owning the direction across PM, engineering, and design, not just shipping the comps. Just on contract.

Accessibility-first feature design

End-to-end design of a feature where accessibility is the starting brief, not a final pass. The Magnifier work is the canonical example: layout decisions came from how low-vision users hold the phone, not from a pattern library.

Design-system work that makes accessibility the default

Component-level audits, plus redesigns of the patterns that turn out to be inaccessible. Every product team that picks up the system gets accessibility for free, not as homework.

Cross-functional alignment at Staff level

Getting PM, engineering, and design to land on the same direction when accessibility constraints push against pattern conventions. Sometimes the case is a video. Sometimes it's a redrawn comp. Sometimes it's just sitting in the room.

Pixel Magnifier

The highest-rated Google-made app on Google Play. 4.8 stars, 154K monthly active users.

The important decisions weren't about the feature list. They were about the layout — clustering all controls at the bottom of the screen so a user holding the phone three inches from their face could reach them without traversing the wall.

02 · Co-Design Facilitation

Co-design with the people the product is actually for.

I bring the structure, the recruiter, the videographer, and a synthesis your product team can use on Monday. These workshops treat disabled users and older adults as design partners, not test subjects.

Workshop design

Session arc, exercises, prompts, materials. Tuned to the population. A workshop with blind users looks different from one with older adults.

Participant recruitment

I work with a contracted recruiter who specializes in disabled and older-adult panels, or with your existing community partnerships. Honoraria, accessibility accommodations, transport planned in, not added later.

Multi-session series

Three days: co-design, design exploration, participants reacting to early concepts. Most sprints skip the reaction day. It's where the depth comes from.

Video documentation

A contracted videographer captures sessions on consent. Stakeholder convincing and team onboarding for years afterward. The close-up perspective insight on Pixel Magnifier was won by video, not by a slide.

Synthesis your team can use

Written report and a working session with your product team. The deliverable I care about is the one that gets you developing the next great experience, not a 60-page deck.

Continuity into product work

If the engagement makes sense to keep going, I stay on as the senior IC designer. If not, I write a clean handoff.

Sessions across the US and internationally, with BLV users, older adults, and neurodiverse populations.

Guided Step

44% fewer obstacle hits. Three continents, 15 BLV participants. Submitted to CHI 2026.

Three rounds of co-design. The 20-participant experimental study also showed 36% fewer total obstacles and 34% fewer near-misses.

Read the Guided Step case study

Simple View

Co-design with older Japanese adults surfaced the confidence gap.

Multi-session series in Tokyo with older adults adopting smartphones. The co-design sessions surfaced a distinction the original product framing missed: the issue wasn't capability, it was confidence. Designing for that distinction is what got the product to up to 80% point-of-sale conversion with carrier partners.

What advisory looks like

Four offerings. Each can stand alone or feed into the next.

A1 · Accessibility program assessment

Diagnostic of where your accessibility practice stands today and where the highest-leverage next investments are. Stakeholder interviews across product, design, engineering, and legal. You get a written report and a presentation to your leadership team.

A2 · Design team coaching and critique

A retained block of time for critique and coaching, structured around your team's actual work. Weekly office hours, periodic group critiques, a written summary of recurring issues for design leadership. Built to lift team capability over time, not to replace internal review.

A3 · Roadmap and prioritization guidance

A focused engagement to sequence accessibility investments across a portfolio. We look at user impact, regulatory exposure, engineering cost, and downstream maintenance. You get a prioritized backlog with rationale, suitable for your existing planning process.

A4 · Pre-engagement design system review

Structured read of an existing design system to identify the highest-impact accessibility improvements. Often a precursor to a full design-system engagement under UX Design.

Engagement model

Scoped to outcomes, not hours.

Every engagement starts with a fit-and-scope conversation, ends with a written deliverable, and includes a closeout review. Pricing is discussed in conversation.

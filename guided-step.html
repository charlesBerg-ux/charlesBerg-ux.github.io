The Challenge

For individuals who are blind or have low vision (BLV), independent navigation presents significant challenges and risks. While guide canes are essential, they don't detect obstacles at trunk or head level. We hypothesized that a smartphone app using AI to detect people and obstacles in real-time could serve as a valuable supplement — increasing safety and confidence without replacing the cane or the user's own judgment.

The Process: A Global Co-Design

We conducted three rounds of iterative co-design and testing with 15 BLV participants around the world, including in Tokyo, Paris, and the US. We tested prototypes in extreme environments like Shibuya station — one of the world's busiest pedestrian areas — gathering feedback through direct observation, interviews, and user journals.

Research participants testing the Guided Step prototype in various environments. Real-world sessions in high-density spaces were essential for understanding the app's effectiveness under actual navigation conditions.

Key Learnings: The Three Pillars

The extensive co-design process revealed three foundational principles for designing assistive navigation technology. These pillars now inform accessibility work across Google.

Guided Setup

For a tool that could be harmful if misused, building trust and proficiency from the first second was non-negotiable.

Minimalist UI

To optimize for screen readers, we eliminated submenus and built a simple, scannable interface with no hidden layers.

Full Customization

Each user preferred a different intensity and type of audio feedback. A highly customizable experience wasn't optional — it was essential.

The Impact: Validated Results

While Guided Step did not move into production, its impact was rigorously validated in an experimental study with 20 BLV participants — the findings of which were submitted to CHI 2026.

The three pillars uncovered during this project — guided setup, minimalist UI, and full customization — provided a strategic framework for future accessibility projects at Google, demonstrating the critical need for deep user partnership when designing for specialized communities.

Google Pixel

Pixel Magnifier

Role

Lead UX Designer

Goal

Create a best-in-class magnifier app to improve accessibility on Pixel devices.

Team

PM, Engineering, UXR, Visual Design

The Challenge

People with low vision rely heavily on their phone's camera to magnify everyday things like menus and bills. We transformed Magnifier from a basic utility into a highly-rated, accessible tool for users with low vision, including features like "viewfinder search" which looks for phrases in real time.

Discovery & Research

Our research involved competitive analysis and partnerships with the Royal Institute for the Blind. A critical insight was that low-vision users hold phones extremely close to their faces, making traditional screen navigation (swiping from edge to edge) a significant hurdle.

Solution & Impact

By clustering all core functionality in a small, predictable area at the bottom of the screen, we made the app far more usable for our target audience. We also introduced "Live Search" to instantly highlight text in the viewfinder.

4.8 Star Rating

The highest rated Google Play store app to date.

Google Pixel

Simple View

Role

Lead UX Designer

Goal

Increase Pixel adoption among older adults in Japan.

Team

PM, Engineering, UXR

The Challenge

The Pixel is a complex device. For older adults, particularly in Japan, this complexity can be a barrier to entry. We designed "Simple View" to make the "front door" of the Pixel more inviting and accessible.

Insights & Design Pivot

Research showed older adults need increased legibility but don't identify as "disabled," making them reluctant to visit Accessibility settings. Our original design was hidden in a menu; we pivoted to bring Simple View directly into the phone's Setup Wizard (SUW), dramatically increasing discoverability.

80% Conversion

Achieved in selected Japanese carrier points of sale.

Google Pixel (Private Beta)

Guided Step

Role

Lead UX Designer

Goal

Create a guide cane supplement for blind users using the Pixel camera.

Team

UXR, PM, Sound Design, Engineering

The Challenge

Guided Step uses the Pixel camera to detect people and objects, providing auditory cues that intensify as obstacles get closer. This is particularly useful in crowded spaces like subway stations where guide canes can't always detect head-level or moving obstacles.

Global Co-Design

We conducted three rounds of iterative co-design with BLV participants in Tokyo, Paris, and the US. Testing in extreme environments like Shibuya station helped us identify three critical pillars for the product: Comprehensive Onboarding, Minimalist UI, and Full Customization.

Nest Home

Nest Home Lights System

Visual: The 4-Dot LED Language

Role

Senior UX Designer

Skill

Systems Thinking & Simplicity

Impact

25M+ Devices, Red Dot Awards

The Challenge

How do you communicate complex device states—listening, thinking, responding, muted, errors—on screenless hardware? I pioneered the 4-dot LED lighting pattern that is now the global standard for the Google Home and Nest ecosystem.

Non-Verbal Semantics

I focused on creating a language that required zero user manual. We used color theory (like Amber for privacy/mute) and pulse rhythms (like breathing for "thinking") to create an instant mental model that works across languages and ages.

Fending Off Complexity

Initial business pressure often involves adding more colors or patterns for specific edge cases. My role was to maintain user clarity through functional minimalism, ensuring the final five patterns covered 99% of user needs effectively.

25M+ Speakers

Pioneered the standard non-verbal UI across all hardware generations.

"The 4-dot system is a masterclass in functional minimalism, communicating state through light as effectively as a screen."

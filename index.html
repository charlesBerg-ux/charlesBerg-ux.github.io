Google Pixel

Pixel Magnifier

Role

Lead UX Designer

Goal

Create a best-in-class magnifier app to improve accessibility on Pixel devices.

Team

PM, Engineering, UXR, Visual Design

The Challenge

People with low vision rely heavily on their phone's camera to magnify everyday things like menus and bills. While functional, it is a makeshift solution. Our competitor, iPhone, had a dedicated Magnifier app, and Pixel needed to provide an equally powerful, accessible, and intuitive tool for its users that could go beyond our competition.

Discovery & Research

Our research involved competitive analysis, institutional partnerships with organizations like the Royal Institute for the Blind, and extensive user interviews. The most surprising insight came from observing users: due to their low vision, they hold the phone extremely close to their faces, often with screen magnification at maximum. This meant that moving from one edge of the screen to another could require multiple swipes, making feature discovery a huge challenge.

Design & Iteration

Testing showed that a "close-up perspective" insight completely changed our design approach. We pivoted to a design that clustered all core functionality in a small, predictable area at the bottom of the screen. While it might look "unbalanced" to a normally-sighted person, it proved to be far more usable and accessible for low-vision users.

Concept A: Rejected

Testing revealed users still struggled with the menu and the small icon sizes.

Concept B: Rejected

This version improved icon sizes, but required too much horizontal travel.

Final Design

Clustering all controls at the bottom proved most accessible.

Solution & Impact

4.8 Star Rating

The highest rated Google Play store app to date.

Nest Home

Nest Home Lights System

Visual Placeholder: Nest Mini 4-Dot LED Animation

Role

Senior UX Designer

Goal

Establish a universal, non-verbal lighting language for the Google Home/Nest ecosystem.

Team

ID, Sound Design, Engineering, PM

The Challenge

Early smart speakers lacked screens, yet needed to communicate complex states: Are you listening? Are you thinking? Did I make a mistake? Is the microphone muted? I was tasked with creating an intuitive light system that worked across different hardware shapes while remaining language-agnostic and accessible to all ages.

The Design Solution: The 4-Dot Language

I pioneered the interaction model for the distinctive 4-dot LED pattern. By focusing on rhythm, color, and motion rather than complex shapes, we created a "digital personality" that felt helpful and approachable.

Simplicity First

The system had to be simple enough for a child to understand and clear enough for an older adult to see from across a room.

Universal Semantics

Using color theory (Amber for privacy/mute) and pulse rhythms (breathing for "thinking") to create an instant mental model.

Impact & Recognition

This lighting language became a hallmark of the Google/Nest brand identity, shipping on over 25 million speakers globally. It stands as a testament to the power of minimalist, non-verbal UX in the home.

25M+ Devices

Standardized across the entire speaker lineup.

"The 4-dot system is a masterclass in functional minimalism, communicating state through light as effectively as a screen."

— Red Dot Design Award Commentary
